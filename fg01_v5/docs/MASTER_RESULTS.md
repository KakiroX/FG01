## 0. Executive summary

**The v5 pivot works, and it works for a different reason than the plan expected.**

The 200 km-shift pivot does lift v4's primary falsification: design-point specific
energy falls from 21.6–179.5 kJ/kg to **12.1–35.7 kJ/kg**, below the 40 kJ/kg
catastrophic threshold at every altitude and for every β in the envelope
(SIM-0, `DERIVED`). That result is clean and robust.

Three further findings changed the shape of the analysis, and two of them are
corrections to the plan's own model rather than confirmations of it:

1. **Missed projectile mass is not orbital debris.** A retrograde intercept at
   any v_rel above Δv_direct(h) leaves the missed mass on a trajectory whose
   perigee is below the atmosphere. At the design point it reenters on its
   first perigee pass, ~45 minutes after release. v4 assumed missed mass stayed
   on a near-circular orbit; that assumption is wrong for this geometry. R6 is
   defused and the FCC/ODMSP disposal rules are satisfied **by construction**
   at every altitude (SIM-2, SIM-10, `DERIVED`).

2. **The guidance wall is an artefact of the sweep-rate assumption, and the real
   constraint is mass, not probability.** v4's miss term `v_rel·τ` assumes the
   target crosses the aim point at the *impact* velocity. The energy gate already
   forces a low-relative-velocity co-orbital engagement, in which the target's
   motion relative to the launcher proceeds at u ≈ 10 m/s while the 619 m/s is
   supplied by the launcher itself. Reproducing v4 exactly gives P_hit = 2.4×10⁻⁵⁸
   at 10 ms lag; the corrected budget gives a **5.8 mm** miss that is *independent
   of latency from 0.1 µs to 100 ms* (SIM-4). Separately, hit/miss is the wrong
   success variable: a target immersed in a shot pattern intercepts only the mass
   incident on its own cross-section, so tolerating a miss costs launched mass as
   σ², not probability. The exact price is `M_launch/m_on_target = π·e·d_miss²/A_target`
   (SIM-3, `DERIVED`).

3. **What binds now is economics and remediation value, not physics.** At the
   selected operating point all four physics constraints pass with probability
   ≈1. The only binding constraint is cost per object, and the dominant Sobol
   term (0.52) is **engagements per platform lifetime** — a quantity this study
   does not derive from first principles. P(viable) at the optimum is **0.67**,
   rising to **0.997 once a platform services ≥10,000 objects** and falling to
   **0** below ~3,000 (SIM-12, SIM-13).

**Honest headline.** P(viable) does *not* reach ~100% on physics alone, and the
plan's own $100,000/kg cost criterion makes it structurally unreachable for
centimetre debris — a 1 cm aluminium sphere masses 1.414 g, so that bar is $141
per object, less than the cost of launching the propellant to reach it. Under a
per-object criterion the concept reaches P(viable) → 0.997 inside a well-defined
envelope. **The concept is not physics-limited; it is limited by the cost of
keeping hardware in orbit and by how many objects one platform can service.**
Against ground-based laser ablation — the only competitor that genuinely
addresses this size class — FG01 remains 3–4 orders of magnitude more expensive
per object, and no part of the v5 pivot addresses that gap.

**Rhenium is not justified.** Momentum is density-independent, so the founding
rationale is void. Tungsten is 73–243× cheaper, 1,037× more available, and its
*higher* area-to-mass ratio at equal grain mass makes missed grains decay faster
— the one axis where density could have favoured rhenium runs against it by 5.7%
(SIM-7).

## 4. SIM-3 — Cloud dispersion and the exported cross-section

**Clohessy–Wiltshire is evaluated exactly (AM-14) and the result is that it does
not matter.** At engagement times-of-flight (≤ 0.25 s for R ≤ 100 m), n·t ≤ 2.8×10⁻⁴
and the exact CW solution differs from straight-line ballistic spreading by
**2.7×10⁻⁸** relative. The cloud cross-section is therefore *not* an
orbital-mechanics outcome at these scales — it is set by the launcher's transverse
velocity dispersion, σ = δv_T·R/v_rel, which is a **design variable**. CW matters
only for release-to-intercept times of minutes, which this engagement does not use.

**The mass consequence (the central new result).** A target of cross-section A_t
immersed in a Gaussian shot pattern of per-axis σ intercepts only the incident
mass. Exactly (non-central χ², equivalently 1 − Marcum Q₁):

> F = ncx2.cdf((R_t/σ)²; df=2, nc=(d/σ)²) → (A_t/2πσ²)·exp(−d²/2σ²)

Minimising launched mass over σ gives **σ\* = d_miss/√2** (numerically 0.7071,
matching the analytic value) and

> **M_launch / m_on_target = π·e·d_miss² / A_target**

| tolerated miss (1 cm target) | mass penalty |
|---|---|
| 1 cm | 10.9× |
| 3 cm | 98.5× |
| 10 cm | 1,090× |
| 30 cm | 9,851× |

**This replaces v4's binary hit/miss collapse with a graceful quadratic cost.**
A larger pattern buys tolerance, but at σ², which is why the concept wants short
range and good pointing rather than a big cloud.

**Mode and grain size.** Grain mass is capped by the requirement that enough
grains strike the target for the delivered momentum to be statistically stable.
At a 10 cm miss tolerance, 1 mg grains (0.45 mm rhenium) put 161 grains on a 1 cm
target (Poisson CV 7.9%) with a per-impact specific energy of **136 J/kg — 0.34%
of the catastrophic threshold**. Recommendation: **discrete grains of 0.1–1 mg**,
not dust (harder to launch coherently, and 10× more conjunction exposure per shot)
and not a single slug.

`outputs/sim03_sigma_cloud_export.csv`.

of propellant** at I_sp 300 s to hold the operating orbit.

**Plane access is the price of the co-orbital geometry (and it is affordable in
RAAN, not in inclination).**

| | cost |
|---|---|
| RAAN, via differential J2 drift from a 50 km parking offset | ~52 m/s round trip, **9.8 d/deg** (i=51.6°) to **43.9 d/deg** (i=82–98°) |
| Inclination | **130 m/s per degree** |

One FG01 therefore serves one inclination band. That is compatible with how debris
actually clusters — breakup families share inclination — but means a **fleet**, not
a single vehicle, for global coverage. Near-polar plane access is slowest exactly
where sun-synchronous debris concentrates.

**Explicit answer to a plan question:** FG01 does *not* burn up with the missed
rhenium. They are separate objects on different trajectories — the grains are on
immediate-reentry ellipses, FG01 stays in its operating orbit and is disposed of
separately (39 kg propellant for direct deorbit from 800 km at 600 kg dry,
I_sp 300 s).

## 14. SIM-13 — Operating point and viability maximisation

Design variables are **chosen** (they are controlled by the programme); only
epistemic uncertainties are sampled. This decomposition is stated explicitly
because it is what makes a high viability number meaningful rather than a claim
about a randomly configured vehicle.

**Optimum: 1 cm debris at 900 km, Δh = 200 km, v_rel = 619 m/s → P(viable) = 0.666**
(natural lifetime 63.5 yr → 18.2 yr after one kick).

**Binding-constraint ledger at the optimum:**

| constraint | P(satisfied) |
|---|---|
| **5 — cost < $50k/object** | **0.663 ← binds** |
| 1 — sub-catastrophic E_s | 0.998 |
| 4 — net debris ≥ 0 | 0.998 |
| 2 — shot mass ≤ 1 kg | 1.000 |
| 3 — post-kick lifetime < 25 yr | 1.000 |
| 6 — remediation value | 1.000 |

**Where P(viable) → ~100%.** Conditioning on the dominant Sobol term:

| engagements per platform lifetime | P(viable) | median cost/object |
|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | 0.000 | $274,446 |
| 3,000 | 0.222 | $91,497 |
| **10,000** | **0.997** | **$27,469** |
| 30,000 | 0.998 | $9,173 |
| 100,000 | 0.998 | $2,772 |

**This is the honest answer to the "P → 100%" objective:** the envelope exists, and
it is defined by a programmatic quantity — **a platform must service ≥10,000
objects in its lifetime**. Below ~3,000 the concept fails outright.

**Robustness.** P(viable) is flat to within 0.01 across control latency (0.1 µs –
100 ms), engagement range (5–100 m), pointing (50–1000 µrad) and muzzle dispersion
(0.1–1%). The only design variable with visible effect is the platform–target
relative speed u, and only weakly (0.661 → 0.651 from 1 to 100 m/s). **Every
engineering parameter that v4 identified as critical is now slack; the constraint
has moved entirely to economics.**

**Solar-activity caveat.** Under a *permanently* fixed F10.7 no altitude is
simultaneously remediation-relevant at solar maximum (the object would decay
unaided) and compliant at solar minimum (the kick is not enough). Fixed F10.7 is
a bounding device, not a multi-decade reality — a real 11-year cycle traverses all
three. Selection is therefore made on the nominal case with the extremes reported.

## 15. Where the gates fail (AM-18 compliance)

Stated in the same place as the successes:

- **Above 900 km**, a single 200 km kick does not bring 1 cm debris inside the
  25-yr rule; above 1000 km it does not for any size tested. Staged kicks fix this
  at linear cost.
- **Below ~700 km**, the kick has no remediation value: the debris decays inside
  25 years unaided.
- **Sub-2 cm debris fails the $100k/kg criterion at every altitude**, under every
  design, by 1–2 orders of magnitude.
- **At β = 1.0 with E_s,c = 30 kJ/kg**, the design point is catastrophic above
  ~520 m/s at 400 km — the safe window narrows but never closes.
- **Below 3,000 engagements per platform lifetime**, P(viable) = 0 everywhere.
- **Against ground-based laser ablation**, FG01 is 3–4 orders of magnitude more
  expensive per object at every operating point examined.
- **No legal regime authorises the operation.**
