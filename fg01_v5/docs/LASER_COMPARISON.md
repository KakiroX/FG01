# FG01 vs. Laser Ablation — The Accuracy Case

**Modules:** LAS-1 (engagement physics), LAS-2 (accuracy head-to-head), LAS-3
(technology readiness, corrected economics). Regenerated by `python run_all.py`.

**Why this exists.** v5 and v6 compared FG01 against a published figure of
$100–500/kg for ground-based laser ablation, tagged "theoretical", and concluded
FG01 loses by five to six orders of magnitude. That comparison was never on the
same footing: FG01's engagement was built from physics while the laser's was a
quoted number nobody re-derived. This document builds the laser engagement from
the same physics, and the conclusion moves substantially.

---

## Executive summary

**The accuracy claim is TRUE and it is large — but one popular version of it is
false, and that version must not be used.**

| | FG01 | Ground laser | favours |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pointing requirement | **876 µrad** | **0.21 µrad** | FG01 **4,115×** |
| Margin over own diffraction limit | **131×** | **1.64×** | FG01 **80×** |
| Target at the sensor | resolved 149×, 2×10¹¹ e⁻/s | unresolved 16×, 4×10⁴ e⁻/s | FG01 |
| Aiming solutions per object | **1** | **294 consecutive** | FG01 |
| External cueing needed | ~1 km | **0.25 m** | FG01 **3,960×** |
| Failure mode on aiming error | graceful — 45% at 2× error | **cliff — zero beyond 1.01×** | FG01 |
| Self-perturbation of target | none | loses track in 10 pulses | FG01 |
| Prime hardware vs. demonstrated | **80,491× inside** | **46× beyond** | FG01 |
| Atmosphere in path | none | turbulence, extinction, blooming | FG01 |
| Duty cycle | continuous | terminator window, weather | FG01 |
| **Cost per 1 cm object** | **$0.9M – 4.2M** | **$381 – $5,702** | **laser, 158–11,000×** |

**The headline correction:** built from physics, a ground laser costs
**$381–$5,702 per 1 cm object**, not the $0.14–0.71 implied by $100–500/kg — a
**536–8,031× correction**. The published figure is a *marginal-energy* cost; the
electricity really is only **$0.67 per object**. It omits the facility, whose
cost is set entirely by the aperture and average power that the accuracy
requirement forces.

**The sharpest single result — the mirror of SIM-6:** the laser needs **93 kW of
average power in nanosecond pulses** on an 8 m adaptive-optics aperture. The
highest demonstrated repetitive nanosecond laser is **2 kW**. The requirement is
**46× beyond the state of the art**, and that is the *best case* across
apertures (a 5 m aperture needs 68×). FG01's launcher, by the identical test,
sits **80,491× inside** demonstrated hardware.

**Revised verdict.** The laser still wins on cost, by two to four orders of
magnitude rather than six. But it wins that comparison only by assuming hardware
that does not exist, pointing at 1.6× its own diffraction limit, tracking an
unresolved point source through turbulence while modelling its own perturbation
of the target. **The honest claim is not "FG01 is cheaper." It is "FG01 is
buildable now and the laser is not, and the laser's cost advantage is contingent
on an accuracy problem with no demonstrated solution."** That is a claim about
risk and timeline, not about price.

## The one argument that must NOT be made

> "The laser needs metre-level orbit prediction; FG01 needs less."

**This is false and it will be caught.** In *linear* terms FG01 needs the tighter
number: it must place a 9 mm cloud on a 1 cm target, against the laser's 25 cm
spot radius. **FG01's linear requirement is 28.8× tighter.**

The claim only works in **angular** terms, and there it is overwhelming — 4,115×
— because the two systems work at ranges differing by five orders of magnitude
(10 m vs. 1,186 km). Pointing hardware, diffraction and atmospheric turbulence
all act on *angles*, not on metres, which is why the angular framing is the
operative one. Any version of the argument that quotes the linear number is
wrong.

**The cleanest and most defensible formulation** avoids the trap entirely:

> Each system's pointing requirement, measured against the diffraction limit of
> its own sensing aperture. **FG01 has 131× of headroom. The laser has 1.64×.**

FG01 works with two orders of magnitude of margin against the physics of its own
optics; the laser must work essentially *at* that limit.

## LAS-1 — The laser's own INT-0: geometry versus optics

Ablation recoil pushes the target **along the line of sight, away from the
illuminator**. For a ground station that is mostly *radial*, and only the
component opposing the target's velocity lowers the orbit:

> retrograde fraction = −R_E·sin(φ) / R

which is **exactly zero at zenith** — the point of shortest range, lowest
airmass and smallest spot — and rises toward the horizon, where range, airmass
and spot size are all worst.

| elevation | range | retrograde fraction | airmass | spot | fluence | Δv/pulse |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10.5° | 2,529 km | **0.862** | 5.33 | 2.09 m | 0.26 J/cm² | **0 — below threshold** |
| 19.1° | 1,992 km | 0.828 | 3.03 | 1.12 m | 1.29 J/cm² | 0.004 m/s |
| 32.0° | 1,489 km | 0.743 | 1.88 | 0.69 m | 4.04 J/cm² | 0.127 m/s |
| **43.7°** | **1,225 km** | **0.634** | 1.45 | **0.53 m** | 7.38 J/cm² | **0.214 m/s** |
| 66.9° | 968 km | 0.344 | 1.09 | 0.39 m | 14.1 J/cm² | 0.161 m/s |
| 82.0° | 908 km | 0.122 | 1.01 | 0.36 m | 16.6 J/cm² | 0.062 m/s |

**The laser cannot have useful geometry and good optics at the same time.** This
is the exact analogue of INT-0's finding for FG01 — and unlike FG01, it has no
co-orbital escape, because a ground station cannot fly alongside the target.

The optimum is a compromise at ~46° elevation that achieves neither: 61% of the
recoil is useful, and the spot is **50× wider than the target**, so **99.96% of
every pulse misses**. The laser pays the same areal penalty FG01 pays — 2,551×
against FG01's 22× — but pays it in photons, which are cheap, rather than in
tungsten, which must be launched. *That is the laser's real advantage, and it is
an advantage about consumables, not about accuracy.*

## LAS-2 — The accuracy asymmetry

### Resolved vs. unresolved, and the photon budget

| sensor | target angular size | diffraction limit | resolved? | magnitude | photoelectrons/s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FG01, 10 cm aperture at 10 m | 1,000 µrad | 6.71 µrad | **yes, 149×** | −7.7 | **2.4×10¹¹** |
| Ground, 5 m at 1,186 km | 0.0084 µrad | 0.134 µrad | **no, 16× below** | +17.6 | 4.2×10⁴ |

FG01 sees an extended object resolved across 149 diffraction widths with seven
orders of magnitude more light. The ground telescope sees an unresolved point
source and must infer position by centroiding.

**An honest correction to a common objection:** detection itself is *feasible* —
SNR ≈ 18 in a 10 ms frame on a 5 m telescope. The claim "a ground telescope
cannot see 1 cm debris" is wrong and should not be made. The difficulty is not
detection; it is that every position measurement is a point-source centroid at
1.6× the diffraction limit, on a target that is moving at 6.2 mrad/s and being
perturbed by the laser itself.

### Graceful degradation vs. threshold cliff

Ablation is a **threshold process**: below F_th the surface is heated and nothing
is ejected, so the impulse is **exactly zero**, not small.

- **FG01 at 2× its nominal aiming error still delivers 44.9% of nominal
  momentum** — a partially-missed cloud still deposits mass.
- **The laser delivers exactly zero beyond 1.01× its tolerance**, when fluence
  crosses the ablation threshold.

The failure modes differ in *kind*, not degree.

### The honest counterpoint, which must be stated

The cliff is **per-pulse**, and the laser fires hundreds of pulses per object, so
it **averages over its own threshold**:

| pointing error (σ, ×tolerance) | laser pulse hit rate | laser pulses needed | laser engagement | FG01 shots needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.25× | 1.00 | 301 | 30 s | 1.03 |
| 0.5× | 0.88 | 491 | 49 s | 1.10 |
| **1.0×** | **0.40** | **1,310** | **131 s** | **1.40** |
| 2.0× | 0.12 | 4,585 | 458 s | 2.60 |
| 4.0× | 0.03 | 17,985 | 1,799 s | 7.47 |

So the cliff **degrades throughput rather than causing outright failure**, until
the engagement no longer fits inside a pass. **Any argument that treats a laser
miss as total mission loss overstates the case.**

What does *not* average out is the tracking requirement: **FG01 needs one aiming
solution; the laser needs 294 consecutive ones.**

### The ablation-feedback problem

Each pulse changes the target's velocity by 176 mm/s. An estimator that does not
model the effect of its own shots walks off the target:

| pulses without feedback | elapsed | unmodelled displacement | fraction of spot radius | lost? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.1 s | 1.1 cm | 0.04 | no |
| 3 | 0.3 s | 9.7 cm | 0.38 | no |
| **10** | **1.0 s** | **1.07 m** | **4.26** | **YES** |

The laser must solve a coupled ablation-and-estimation problem in real time for
the entire engagement. **FG01 fires once and has no such coupling.**

### Point-ahead

| system | point-ahead angle | tolerance | fractional accuracy needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground laser | 49.4 µrad | 0.21 µrad | **0.43%** |
| FG01 | 16,155 µrad | 876 µrad | 5.4% |

The laser's lead angle is small but its tolerance is smaller — it must compute
the lead **12.6× more accurately**.

## LAS-3 — Technology readiness and corrected economics

### Does the required laser exist?

| aperture | spot | pulse energy | average power | vs. demonstrated (2 kW) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 m | 0.77 m | 28.7 kJ | 287 kW | **143×** |
| 5 m | 0.53 m | 13.5 kJ | 135 kW | **68×** |
| **8 m** | **0.44 m** | **9.3 kJ** | **93 kW** | **46× ← best case** |
| 10 m | 0.44 m | 9.5 kJ | 95 kW | 48× |
| 15 m | 0.61 m | 18.1 kJ | 181 kW | 90× |

There is an interior optimum near 8–10 m: below it the spot grows by
diffraction, above it adaptive-optics Strehl collapses (D/r₀ rises faster than
actuators can follow).

Demonstrated systems: LLNL Mercury (100 J @ 10 Hz), DiPOLE-100 (100 J @ 10 Hz),
HAPLS (200 J @ 10 Hz) — **2 kW average is the state of the art.**

> **The laser is 46× beyond demonstrated hardware. FG01's launcher is 80,491×
> inside it.** These are the same test applied to each concept's prime hardware,
> and they point in opposite directions.

### Corrected cost per object

| aperture | facility | throughput | **cost/object** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 m | $157M | 265k–412k / decade | **$381 – $592** |
| 10 m | $605M | 265k–412k / decade | $1,469 – $2,285 |
| 15 m | $1.51B | 265k–412k / decade | $3,665 – $5,702 |

Throughput is limited by the terminator window (the target must be sunlit while
the site is dark), by weather, and by 30 s of engagement plus 60 s of
acquisition overhead per object.

**Correction to the published figure: 536–8,031×.** The $100–500/kg figure
captures only marginal energy — genuinely $0.67 per object — and omits the
facility that the accuracy requirement forces.

### The shared prerequisite neither system has

Both need a catalogue of the ≥1 cm population that does not exist (~40,000
tracked of 1.2 million). But the laser must be **handed** a 0.25 m solution at
1,186 km and has no means of improving on it, while FG01 needs only km-level
cueing and then closes the loop with its own sensor at 10¹¹ photoelectrons/s.
**The laser needs the catalogue to be 3,960× more accurate.**

## What this does and does not establish

**Establishes:** FG01's accuracy requirement is 4,115× looser in angle, has 80×
more margin against its own optics, needs one aiming solution instead of 294,
degrades gracefully instead of falling off a cliff, requires no atmospheric
correction, has no self-perturbation feedback loop, and — decisively — is built
from hardware that exists today while the laser's is 46× beyond it.

**Does not establish:** that FG01 is cheaper. It is not; the laser retains a
158–11,000× cost advantage per object even after the correction. And **none of
this repairs FG01's own binding constraint.** ECO-1's Δv-per-engagement
invariant is untouched: FG01 still services only ~24 objects per platform-decade,
and that — not the laser benchmark — is what makes small-debris removal
uneconomic for it. *Being more buildable than a competitor that also does not
work is a weak claim on its own.*

**Where the argument is strongest.** Not at 1 cm. FG01's areal penalty collapses
to 1 by 3 cm and stays there, while the laser's problem gets strictly *worse*
with target size — fluence is energy per unit **area**, so a larger target needs
proportionally more total impulse with no relief on the fluence requirement.
Combined with ECO-5's finding that the momentum kick is only cost-competitive
against a tug between ~100 kg and ~3.7 t, **the accuracy argument and the
economic argument point at the same target class: tens to hundreds of kilograms,
roughly 30–70 cm.** That is the only regime where FG01 is simultaneously more
buildable than a laser and cheaper than a tug.

## LAS-4 — The space-based laser: the strongest counterargument, answered

The obvious rebuttal to everything above is: **put the laser in orbit.** No
atmosphere, no airmass, no turbulence, no adaptive optics, no terminator window,
no elevation tension. This was flagged as the largest gap in the comparison
(L26). It is now closed, and the answer is **not** the one that flatters FG01
most — it must be conceded on one axis and it is decisive on two others.

### 1. Does it escape the optics problem? **Yes, completely.**

| range | spot (1 m aperture, 1.06 µm) | on target | pulse energy | pulses | average power |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 m | 0.34 mm | **100%** | 0.004 J | 164,971 | 0.04 W |
| 1 km | 3.4 mm | **100%** | 0.44 J | 1,650 | 4.4 W |
| **10 km** | **3.4 cm** | 8.8% | 44 J | **187** | **444 W** |
| 100 km | 34 cm | 0.09% | 4.4 kJ | 187 | 44 kW |

At short range the spot falls **below the target size** and all the energy
lands. A 1 m aperture at 10 km needs **444 W of average power** against the
ground laser's **93 kW** — the 46× technology gap **disappears entirely**. This
is a space-qualifiable laser, not a national-facility one.

**Any argument for FG01 that rests on the ground laser's optics problem does not
survive this.** Concede it explicitly rather than be caught by it.

### 2. Does it escape the delivery geometry? **No.**

Ablation recoil points **along the line of sight, away from the illuminator**.
So the illuminator must lie *ahead of the target along its own velocity vector*:

| illuminator position relative to target | useful retrograde fraction | effect |
|---|---|---|
| **directly ahead (co-orbital, leading)** | **1.000** | retrograde — lowers orbit |
| ahead, 10° above | 0.985 | retrograde |
| ahead, 45° above | 0.707 | retrograde |
| directly above / below | **0.000** | no along-track component at all |
| abeam (different plane) | **0.000** | no along-track component at all |
| directly behind (trailing) | −1.000 | **prograde — RAISES the orbit** |

A laser in a higher orbit, a lower orbit, or a different plane delivers **zero**
useful impulse. The efficient geometry for an orbiting laser is **exactly the
co-orbital leading geometry INT-0 derived for a kinetic impactor** — the two
methods are constrained identically, because the constraint comes from *which
way the momentum must point*, not from how it is delivered.

### 3. Does it escape the economics? **No.**

| architecture | must visit targets? | Δv per engagement | Δv for 10⁴ engagements |
|---|---|---|---|
| FG01 kinetic kick | yes | **106.9 m/s** | 1,069 km/s |
| **Space laser, co-orbital** | **yes** | **106.9 m/s** | **1,069 km/s** |
| Ground laser | **no** | **0** | 0 — Earth's rotation delivers the targets |

Because it must be co-orbital to push retrograde, it must visit its targets, and
ECO-1's invariant applies **unchanged**. A space laser services the same **~24
objects per platform-decade** as FG01.

### Where the space laser genuinely beats FG01: consumables

| engagements | FG01 tungsten | FG01 consumable cost | laser consumable |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24 | 51 g | $159 | **$0** |
| 1,000 | 2.1 kg | $6,634 | **$0** |
| 10,000 | **21.4 kg** | $66,340 | **$0** |

A laser converts sunlight and has an **effectively infinite magazine** — 8.3 s
of solar charging per object at 5 kW. FG01 carries tungsten and runs out.
**If the Δv problem were ever solved, the space laser would be the better
architecture and FG01's magazine would become its binding limit.**

### Where FG01 still wins

**Pointing, by 521×** — and this holds at *every* range, because the laser's spot
and its range scale together, fixing its angular tolerance at **1.68 µrad**
regardless of standoff, against FG01's **876 µrad**. FG01's effective "spot" is a
physical cloud that can be *sized to the expected miss distance*; the laser's is
set by diffraction and cannot be widened without losing fluence. FG01 also keeps
graceful degradation and one aiming solution instead of hundreds.

### The unifying result

> **Momentum must point retrograde. Anything that delivers retrograde momentum
> must approach from ahead along the velocity vector. That forces co-orbital
> geometry, which forces the Δv invariant.**

Ablation, kinetic impact and a tug are all subject to it. **The only escape is to
stay on the ground and let Earth's rotation do the transporting** — and that
escape costs a 46× technology gap and an unavoidable geometry penalty. **No
configuration escapes both.** This is a statement about small-debris remediation
in general, not about FG01.

### Effect on the argument

It **narrows** it. Against a *ground* laser the accuracy case is strong on every
axis. Against a *space* laser only **pointing and readiness** survive, and the
**consumables axis reverses**. Make the argument against the ground laser — which
is the system the published $100–500/kg figure actually describes — and concede
the space-laser case openly.

## Caveats

- **C_m = 10⁻⁴ N·s/J** is the standard peak coupling for ns pulses on metals; a
  factor-2 error moves the laser's pulse count and average power proportionally.
  `SOURCED, laser-propulsion literature`
- **F_th = 1 J/cm², F_opt = 5 J/cm²** for Al. The threshold spans 0.5–5 J/cm² in
  the literature; the cliff *position* moves with it, its *existence* does not.
  `SOURCED range`
- **AO model** is deliberately generous: fitting error from 1,000 actuators, not
  the full D/r₀ variance, giving Strehl 0.35–0.96. A pessimistic AO model would
  strengthen the FG01 case, so the favourable choice was made deliberately.
  `DERIVED`
- **Facility costs** ($157M–1.5B) and **throughput** (terminator window, weather,
  overhead) are parametric. `UNVALIDATED`
- **Space-based lasers** are now modelled in LAS-4, and the anticipation above
  was half right: they do inherit the transportation problem and the economics
  do not improve, but the accuracy asymmetry narrows further than expected —
  the optics advantage passes entirely to the laser, and the consumables axis
  reverses. Only pointing (521×) and readiness survive.
- **LAS-4 does not model the space laser's thermal or power system.** 444 W of
  average optical output at ~20% wall-plug means ~1.8 kW of waste heat to
  radiate continuously during an engagement, on a platform that must also
  station-keep. This is the most likely place the space-laser case degrades,
  and it is not computed here. `UNVALIDATED`
