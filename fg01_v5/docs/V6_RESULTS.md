## Executive summary

**Part I (interaction physics): the model survives being resolved, with one
correction that darkens it and one that brightens it.**

The single-impactor stand-in has been replaced by a resolved grain swarm.
AM-15/AM-16 are discharged — computed, not assumed. Two corrections roughly
cancel:

- **β is 1.20, not 1.5** (INT-2). Derived from crater-ejecta scaling rather
  than assumed. Al-6061's strength velocity is 320 m/s, so a 619 m/s impact is
  only 1.9× the speed at which the target can eject anything, and almost no mass
  leaves fast enough to enhance momentum. **Required mass rises 1.25×.** The same
  model at 6 km/s reproduces the hypervelocity β ≈ 2–3 the literature reports,
  which is the consistency check.
- **A Gaussian cloud is 3× more mass-efficient than the plan assumed** (INT-1).
  ε = 2.995·(d_t/d_cloud)², not (d_t/d_cloud)², because a peaked profile
  concentrates mass where the target is.

Net: the plan's worked point (≈3 g, $22–30) becomes **1.75 g launched → 197 mg
on target → 197 grains, $17.95 rhenium / $5.41 tungsten.** Bulk E_s = 26.7 kJ/kg
(0.67× threshold); per-grain E_s = 136 J/kg (0.34% of threshold).

**INT-0 upholds the co-orbital premise — but relocates the reason.** The premise
is not a guidance claim at all; it is forced by kinematics before guidance is
considered. A crossing engagement wastes all but sin(θ/2) of its relative
velocity on a cross-track impulse that changes inclination rather than altitude,
while paying the full |w|² in disruption energy. Delivering the same 51.8 m/s
retrograde kick from *any* crossing angle costs **333.5 kJ/kg — 8.3× the
catastrophic threshold** — against 13.9 kJ/kg co-orbital. Co-orbital wins by
**2·v_orbital/|w| = 23.9×**. Verified against explicit 3-D states to 2×10⁻¹⁶.

**Part II (economics): the architecture fails, decisively, and not for any
reason the plan anticipated.**

ECO-2 found ~8,900 fragments per decade whose orbital plane sweeps through the
firing cone at Fengyun-1C. ECO-1 then charged the Δv to actually reach them and
**8,900 opportunities collapsed to ~24 engagements.** A fragment in the
platform's plane but at a different altitude is unreachable: two circular orbits
at different altitudes never come close.

The reason is a closed-form invariant, derived and validated to 1.14%:

> **Δv per engagement = π·v_orbital / (3.5·Ω̇·T_mission)**

Widening the altitude band raises opportunities linearly (differential nodal
rate) and raises hop cost linearly (Hohmann Δv). **They cancel exactly.** The
cost per engagement cannot be tuned — only orbital velocity, nodal regression
rate and mission duration enter, none of which the designer controls once the
cluster is chosen. At Fengyun-1C over 10 years it is **107 m/s per engagement**.
Reaching 10,000 engagements would cost **1,070 km/s**.

**Two further findings kill the "cluster" premise itself:**

1. **Every cloud studied has spread to a full 360° in RAAN.** Differential nodal
   regression turns a compact breakup cloud into a complete shell within a few
   years. What survives is a shared *inclination band*, not a shared plane. The
   co-orbital cluster the plan hoped to service does not exist.
2. **A hard inclination filter.** Since θ² ≈ (Δi)² + (sin i·ΔΩ)², a fragment
   whose *inclination* differs by more than the firing cone can never be engaged,
   at any RAAN, at any time, for any Δv. A breakup imparts an isotropic velocity
   spread, so most of a cloud is permanently out of reach.
3. **Sun-synchronous is the worst case.** Ω̇ ∝ cos(i), so orbits near 98° have the
   slowest nodal regression: 107 m/s/engagement at Fengyun-1C against 27 m/s for
   a hypothetical 51.6° cluster. **The most valuable debris sits in the worst
   place for this architecture.**

**Cost per 1 cm object: $0.9M–4.2M** against the plan's $5,000 niche target — a
shortfall of ~200–800×. Fleet scaling does not help: engagements scale linearly
with platforms, so cost per object falls only as the learning curve allows
(1,000 platforms → $412k/object, and 24,000 objects is 2% of the population).

**Part IV verdict: conditions 1, 2, 5, 6 PASS; conditions 3 and 4 FAIL.**

## The constructive result — and its boundary (ECO-5)

The kick physics is sound and **target-agnostic**. Applying it to larger objects —
same launcher, same 619 m/s intercept, same sub-catastrophic specific energy
(26.9 kJ/kg, identical because E_s is mass-independent) — improves the economics:

| | small debris (1 cm) | SL-8 upper stage (1.4 t) |
|---|---|---|
| Areal mass penalty | 21.7× | **1.0** (aim is a non-issue) |
| Launched mass per object | 2.14 g | 197 kg |
| Δv to visit | 107 m/s | 107 m/s — *the same* |
| Cost per object | $0.9M–4.2M | **$5.9M** (platform scaled) |
| Cost per kg removed | $6.4×10⁸ | **$4,235** |

### But "competitive" is not "efficient", and the distinction decides the case

ECO-4 compared against ADR-tug *programme* costs and did not scale the platform
to carry its own magazine. ECO-5 asks the prior question — how efficiently does a
momentum kick deliver impulse at all? — and the answer is a single number:

> **v_eff = β·v_rel = 743 m/s → equivalent I_sp = 75.8 s**

**Worse than cold gas.** A rocket ejects propellant at its exhaust velocity; FG01
ejects tungsten at the intercept velocity. So the projectile mass is a *fixed
fraction of the target's mass*, independent of target size:

> **m_projectile / m_target = γ·Δv / (β·v_rel) = 14%**

Invisible for a 1.4 g fragment (0.2 mg of tungsten). Decisive for an 8.3 t stage
(1.17 t of tungsten to launch).

**There is a genuine interior optimum**, from two opposing effects:

| target mass | FG01 vs. an electric tug of equal platform cost |
|---|---|
| below ~56 kg | **FG01 cheaper** — a tug must accelerate its own ~500 kg; the projectile carries no dead mass |
| 100 kg | +$0.02M (break-even) |
| 1.4 t | +$0.74M |
| 8.3 t | +$4.89M |

Delivering 51.8 m/s to a 1 cm fragment costs 0.2 mg of tungsten against 0.89 kg
of xenon — the kick is *most* mass-efficient exactly where the target is
smallest, and that is precisely where the tour Δv makes it uneconomic anyway.

**A term neither the plan nor ECO-4 accounted for:** firing the magazine
retrograde pushes the platform prograde. For 10 SL-16 engagements the magazine is
11.7 t and the **recoil Δv is 789 m/s** — larger than the entire tour budget,
costing 976 kg of xenon to cancel. Recoil scales with magazine mass, so it is a
third penalty growing with target mass.

### Revised verdict

**Not efficient — competitive within a bounded window.** Roughly **100 kg to
~3.7 t**, and only if avoiding docking is worth a few million dollars per object,
which is a programmatic judgement this study cannot settle. Above ~3.7 t the 14%
projectile fraction plus recoil dominates and the case fails against a tug.

The sweet spot is **not** the multi-tonne upper stages ECO-4 highlighted. It is
intermediate objects of **tens to hundreds of kilograms** — too big for a laser,
too small to justify a bespoke tug, tumbling enough to make docking unattractive.

*(This supersedes the ECO-4 claim of "competitive against large tracked objects",
which was measured against the wrong quantity.)*

## The value side

Removing one randomly-selected ≥1 cm fragment avoids **$67–$20,124** of expected
asset loss across the swept assumptions, and **at most ~$67** in the subset
consistent with the observed loss record. Against $0.9M–4.2M per object the gap
is **10²–10⁵×**.

The value model is reported with its own reality check: at full shell overlap it
implies ~80 debris losses per year across the active fleet, and the observed
record shows essentially none — so the model over-predicts and the low end is the
credible one. This is not a failing of FG01 specifically. It is why operational
ADR targets large objects, whose removal prevents the breakups that create
fragments in the first place. **The case for small-debris removal has to be made
on systemic cascade prevention, which this study does not model.**

## Part I — module results

### INT-0 — Engagement geometry (the validation gate)

**Validation:** closed forms against explicit 3-D inertial states at a common
node — |w| to 2.1×10⁻¹⁶, cos ψ to 2.8×10⁻¹¹.

| approach | \|w\| (m/s) | cos ψ | E_s for the same kick | vs threshold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| co-orbital, launcher-supplied | 619 | 1.000 | 13.9 kJ/kg | 0.35× |
| crossing, 0.1° | 12.9 | 0.00087 | 333.5 kJ/kg | 8.3× |
| crossing, 8.5° | 1,097 | 0.0741 | 333.5 kJ/kg | 8.3× |
| crossing, 90° | 10,471 | 0.707 | 333.5 kJ/kg | 8.3× |

E_s is **independent of crossing angle** — a consequence of
useful-Δv-per-specific-energy = β/v_orbital for any crossing, against 2β/|w|
co-orbital.

**The operational case** (nearly co-orbital, plane offset θ): E_s grows as
1 + (v_geom/v_launch)². Sub-catastrophic out to θ = 5°; ≤10% energy penalty out
to **θ = 1.5°**, which is the firing cone handed to ECO-1.

**Premise #3 verdict: UPHELD, with the boundary stated.** The correct statement
is not "lag does not matter" but *"the only geometry that transfers momentum
efficiently is also the geometry in which lag does not matter."* Nothing
downstream depends on the disputed guidance claim, because crossing engagements
are excluded on kinematics first.

Secondary finding: leading the target is **mandatory, not optional** — even at
zero latency, a non-extrapolating aim law tolerates only 0.19–0.31 m/s of
transverse rate.

### INT-1 — Cloud areal-density field

Validated against grain-by-grain Monte Carlo (adaptive sampling, ≥2000 expected
hits per case): agreement to **2.16 MC standard errors**.

| cloud diameter | ε on 1 cm target | plan's uniform-disc estimate |
|---|---|---|
| 2 cm | 0.527 | 0.25 |
| 5 cm | 0.113 | 0.04 |
| 10 cm | 0.0295 | 0.01 |
| 20 cm | 0.0075 | 0.0025 |

Convention stated explicitly (the plan did not fix one): d_cloud is the
95%-mass-containment diameter, σ = d_cloud/4.895.

**Recommendation:** 200–450 µm grains. At 450 µm a 3 g shot puts 338 grains on a
1 cm target through a 5 cm cloud — above the 100-grain floor for Poisson CV < 10%.
Smaller grains improve statistics but multiply released-grain count and transient
conjunction exposure; larger grains raise per-impact energy.

### INT-2 — β from physics

| v_imp (m/s) | β low | β central | β high | β oblique | regime |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 400 | 1.055 | 1.094 | 1.199 | 1.063 | transitional |
| **619** | **1.170** | **1.301** | **1.681** | **1.201** | transitional |
| 1000 | 1.307 | 1.568 | 2.394 | 1.378 | hypervelocity |
| 6000 | 1.957 | 3.072 | 8.266 | 2.381 | hypervelocity |

**Recommended for downstream: β = 1.201** (oblique-corrected for a curved target,
⟨cos i⟩ = 2/3).

Consequence: mass on target rises from 158 mg to 197 mg (1.25×); E_s rises from
21.4 to 26.7 kJ/kg — from 0.53× to 0.67× the threshold. At the pessimistic end
(pure embedding, β = 1.0) E_s reaches 32.1 kJ/kg, which **breaches the 30 kJ/kg
lower-bound threshold**.

**Embedding finding:** 450 µm rhenium penetrates 1.46 mm into aluminium at
619 m/s — 3.2× its own diameter, far short of perforating a 1 cm target. Grains
embed, which is the physical floor β = 1 and why the answer sits so close to it.

**AM-4a status:** β moves from UNVALIDATED toward DERIVED — computed from a
sourced scaling law and validated against the hypervelocity regime. Not yet
SOURCED: no direct measurement at Al-on-Al, sub-km/s, cm-scale exists.

### INT-3/4 — Aggregate delivery and aim tolerance

Worked point (1 cm at 900 km, 5 cm cloud, β = 1.201, 619 m/s): **1.75 g launched
→ 197 mg on target → 197 grains.**

Δv distribution per shot (aim scatter + Poisson lumpiness, γ = 2 sizing):

| cloud | m_launch | Δv p05 | p50 | p95 | meets requirement |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 cm | 0.37 g | 1.1 | 41.9 | 105 | 42% |
| 5 cm | 1.75 g | 43.7 | 88.0 | 118 | **91%** |
| 10 cm | 6.68 g | 81.0 | 103 | 124 | 99.98% |

Dominated by aim scatter, not Poisson lumpiness — at 200+ grains the swarm
averaging is effectively deterministic. This is the quantitative form of the PI's
"many grains touching the debris" picture.

**Methodological note:** γ = 2 and "deliver on 95% of shots" are two ways of
buying the same margin; applying both double-counts. INT-4 sizing uses γ = 1 with
the percentile carrying the margin, cross-checked by Monte Carlo (94.7% achieved
against a 95% target).

**Velocity-ceiling correction.** The v6 plan's Part III proposes 1,000–1,100 m/s
("upper safe-zone, less launched mass"). Recomputed at 900 km with the INT-2 β:

| v_rel | β | E_s (kJ/kg) | vs 40 | vs 30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 619 | 1.201 | 26.7 | PASS | PASS |
| 800 | 1.292 | 32.1 | PASS | **FAIL** |
| 1000 | 1.378 | 37.6 | PASS | **FAIL** |
| 1100 | 1.417 | 40.2 | **FAIL** | **FAIL** |

The proposed operating point is **marginal, not safe**. Two effects partly cancel
(lower β needs more mass; β itself rises with speed), so the ceiling drops less
than the raw β change suggests — but the recommendation is to stay at ≤619 m/s.
Raising v_rel to save consumable mass is a false economy when consumables are
~$5/shot.

### INT-5 — Cloud ejecta and net debris

197 craters excavate 370 mg (26% of target mass). Largest crater **1.39 mm** —
7× below the 1 cm trackable threshold.

| threshold | item | count |
|---|---|---|
| >1 cm | target removed | +1 |
| >1 cm | ejecta (conservative l_max = full crater) | **0, identically** |
| >1 cm | embedded grains, missed grains | 0 |
| **>1 cm** | **NET** | **+1** |
| >1 mm | ejecta, conservative / expected bound | −17.4 / 0 |

The >1 mm line is reported but not netted: sub-mm particles are not tracked
objects and decay in under 2 years (10 µm ejecta in 0.02 yr against 17.6 yr for
the parent).

This is a **stronger** statement than v5's, which argued from the sub-catastrophic
regime; here it follows from crater geometry directly — production terminates at
the crater diameter.

### ECO-3 — Platform and consumables

Minimised platform (400 kg dry, $50k/kg build, $3k/kg launch): **$21.2M** single

**Cost-structure inversion.** v5 found cost was 99.4% platform amortisation at an

projectile, material, launcher or grain size has any material effect on cost per
object.** The plan's concern that rhenium supply would force tungsten at N ≥ 10⁵
is moot — the supply argument evaporates with the engagement count, though
tungsten remains right on cost and physics.

## Risks, updated

- **R1′ (INT-0 reversal is the most attackable claim)** — *defused*. The claim no
  longer carries the argument: crossing engagements are excluded on kinematics
  (8.3× threshold at every angle) before guidance is considered.
- **R2′ (ECO-2 member count is modelled, not catalogued)** — *stands, and is now
  moot*. Only ~3% of the ≥1 cm population is tracked. But the binding constraint
  turned out to be Δv per engagement, not member count, so the uncertainty in the
  count no longer drives the verdict.
- **R3′ (naming: "rhenium" while economics force tungsten)** — *stands*, and is
  now doubly awkward: at 24 engagements per mission the consumable is 51 g and
  the material choice is economically irrelevant either way.
- **R8′ (benchmarking against theoretical lasers is unfair both ways)** —
  *superseded*. Architecture B has a **demonstrated-class** benchmark (ADR tugs,
  $10M–100M/object) and beats it.
- **NEW R11 — the engagement-cadence assumption.** ECO-1 charges 8 h per
  engagement and the tour is Δv-limited before it is cadence-limited, so this
  assumption is not load-bearing *at the realised counts*. It would become
  load-bearing in any architecture that solved the Δv problem.
- **NEW R12 — the value model spans 3 orders of magnitude** and its upper end
  conflicts with the observed loss record. The cost/value gap is robust
  (≥10²× everywhere in the sweep), but the precise ratio is not.

## What would change the answer

1. **A fundamentally different visiting mode.** The Δv invariant is the whole
   result. Anything that removes the need to match altitude per target — which
   INT-0 shows cannot be done with a momentum kick, since a retrograde impulse
   requires travelling anti-parallel to the target's velocity — would reopen the
   economics.
2. **Retargeting at large objects (Architecture B).** Already competitive; needs
   a proper mass-budget and magazine study, since 197–1,166 kg of tungsten per
   object is a large platform.
3. **A β measurement** at Al-on-Al, cm-scale, 0.5–1 km/s. Still the widest
   physics uncertainty, now narrowed from U(1.0, 2.5) to 1.17–1.68.
4. **A systemic cascade-value model.** The per-object value framing is what
   condemns small-debris removal; the cascade-prevention framing is the only one
   under which it could pay, and it is not modelled here.
